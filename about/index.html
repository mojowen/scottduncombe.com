About

Where I come from.

I like building tools for folks doing good work.

I grew up in Oregon and Singapore and went to school at the University of Chicago, collecting a degree in Chemistry and Political Science. My first website was the official Student Government blog — built to fulfill a campaign promise from my run for Student Government President.

After graduating I went to work as an organizer for Obama for America in New Hampshire's remote Carroll County. After Carroll County went blue for the first time since the 1950s, I headed back west to organize at Focus the Nation, a nonprofit dedicated to empowering young people to do something about climate change. One day the website broke and I fixed it — and I guess the rest has been downhill from there: I dove head-first into teaching myself how to code, fork, hack, and debug. Code and bits became my vocation.

From Focus I worked at the Oregon Bus Project and its national branch, the Bus Federation, as a data & technology manager — and started freelancing with friends in my spare time, helping clients create affordable, quick, and beautiful solutions for their web and organizing problems.

In 2013 I moved to San Francisco to be the lead developer at ShareProgress; then Counsyl, on a 50-person engineering team improving the experience for patients and doctors; then Karius, a very small genomics startup, exposing and visualizing genomic data for a team of molecular biologists and a staff physician.

In August 2015 I moved home to Portland, Oregon. Since then: I co-founded Pizza to the Polls (2016 — still feeding voters), helped launch Power the Polls (2020 — 710,939 poll workers), and co-founded MoodMonitor (2016–2020 — merged into Osmind).

Lately I spend my days orchestrating a small fleet of autonomous coding agents that build and run much of the infrastructure behind Pizza to the Polls — the sites, the services, the workflows, and the robots that watch the robots.

What I can do

I mostly make tools for the web — front ends, back ends, data pipelines, static sites, and increasingly: fleets of agents that build them alongside me. Historically WordPress; these days JavaScript/TypeScript, serverless on AWS, Zapier fleets, GitHub Actions — with a long history in Ruby on Rails and Python/Django — and whatever gets the good work done fastest.
